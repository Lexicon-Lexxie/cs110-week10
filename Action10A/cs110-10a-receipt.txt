## R1 - Game research
Game: Half-Life (1998)

Goal: Survive the catastrophic "Resonance Cascade" incident, fight through hostile alien lifeforms and military cleanup squads across the Black Mesa Research Facility, and ultimately travel to the alien borderworld of Xen to defeat the Nihilanth and seal the interdimensional rift.

Player action: First-person movement (running, crouch-jumping, ladder climbing), navigating environmental hazards, weapon management and combat (using the crowbar, firearms, and experimental energy weapons), and interacting with the environment (pushing objects, pressing buttons, turning valves).

Feedback: It expanded as an engine, influencing many other games, and the making of new engine.

Source link: https://store.steampowered.com/app/70/HalfLife/

## R2 - Three engines and my choice
1. Unity / 2D and 3D / C# / Massive multiplatform export support and a huge ecosystem of Asset Store tools and packages / https://unity.com

2. Godot / 2D and 3D / GDScript, C# / Lightweight, completely open-source node-and-scene tree hierarchy with no royalties / https://godotengine.org

3. GoldSrc / 3D / C and C++ / Robust client-server networked multiplayer architecture and BSP map geometry / https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/GoldSource

I would try Unity because it has an enormous library of learning resources, extensive community support, and uses industry-standard C# which makes troubleshooting and building projects much easier
